
## Classification Procedure

Let $A$ be a complex abelian variety of dimension six. The first split is between simple and non-simple varieties, always understood up to isogeny.

For every codimension $p$, write the space of rational Hodge classes as
$$
\mathcal B^p(A)
=H^{2p}(A,\mathbb Q)\cap H^{p,p}(A).
$$
Let $\mathcal D^\bullet(A)$ be the graded subalgebra generated by divisor classes $\mathcal B^1(A)$. The classification problem is therefore to determine
$$
\mathcal B^\bullet(A),\qquad
\mathcal E^p(A)=\mathcal B^p(A)/\mathcal D^p(A),
$$
where $\mathcal E^p(A)\neq 0$ records the presence of exceptional Hodge classes.

For **simple** $A$, compute the rational endomorphism algebra
$$
D=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)=\operatorname{End}(A)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q
$$
together with its Rosati involution. Use the Albert classification:

1. **Type I:** $D$ is a totally real number field and the involution is the identity.
2. **Type II:** $D$ is a totally indefinite quaternion algebra over a totally real field.
3. **Type III:** $D$ is a totally definite quaternion algebra over a totally real field.
4. **Type IV:** $D$ is a division algebra over a CM field and the involution is of the second kind.

Set
$$
V=H_1(A,\mathbb Q),\qquad F=Z(D),\qquad [D:F]=d^2.
$$
If $m=\dim_DV$, the first numerical filter is
$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V=12=m\,d^2[F:\mathbb Q].
$$
Each Albert type must therefore be refined by the center $F$, the degree $d$, the relative dimension $m$, polarization data, and the action on $\operatorname{Lie}(A)$. Only parameter combinations satisfying this dimension constraint are retained.

If $\varphi$ is the alternating form induced by a polarization, then the Hodge group satisfies
$$
\operatorname{Hg}(A)\subseteq
\operatorname{Sp}_D(V,\varphi)
=\{g\in\operatorname{Sp}(V,\varphi):gd=dg\text{ for every }d\in D\}.
$$
For every surviving subtype, compute
$$
\mathcal B^p(A)
=H^{2p}(A,\mathbb Q)^{\operatorname{Hg}(A)}
=\left(\bigwedge^{2p}V^*\right)^{\operatorname{Hg}(A)}.
$$
Then compare this invariant space with $\mathcal D^p(A)$. If Weil classes form a subspace $\mathcal W^p(A)$, the still-unexplained part is measured by
$$
\mathcal B^p(A)\big/\bigl(\mathcal D^p(A)+\mathcal W^p(A)\bigr).
$$
If a subtype does not determine $\operatorname{Hg}(A)$ or its invariants uniquely, refine it further rather than treating an inconclusive computation as a failure.

For **non-simple** $A$, write its isogeny decomposition as
$$
A\sim\prod_i B_i^{n_i}, \qquad \sum_i n_i\dim B_i=6,
$$
where the $B_i$ are pairwise non-isogenous simple abelian varieties. Organize the search first by partitions of six, such as $1+5$, $2+4$, $3+3$, $1+1+4$, and their refinements; then classify every simple factor by Albert type and record multiplicities and possible Hom-relations. Compute the product Hodge group and its invariant classes, including mixed classes arising between factors.

The Künneth decomposition gives
$$
H^k(A,\mathbb Q)
\cong
\bigoplus_{\sum_i k_i=k}
\bigotimes_i H^{k_i}\!\left(B_i^{n_i},\mathbb Q\right),
$$
while the Hodge group has surjective projections through an inclusion
$$
\operatorname{Hg}(A)
\hookrightarrow
\prod_i\operatorname{Hg}\!\left(B_i^{n_i}\right).
$$
Equality must be proved rather than assumed. If the inclusion is strict, invariant tensors coupling different factors may produce mixed exceptional or Weil classes.

Finally, record degeneration or specialization relations between cases. A difficult decomposition may be reduced to an already solved case, while a failed computation should be stored with its precise obstruction: it may eliminate an entire family, indicate a missing refinement, or suggest a nearby construction. Routine cases should be discharged automatically; unresolved exceptional cases should be promoted for discussion by the human mathematicians.

## Relationship Between the Four- and Six-Dimensional Cases

The four-dimensional classification provides the template for dimension six. In both dimensions, one first separates simple from non-simple abelian varieties. Schematically, the two classification trees begin with
$$
4=4\quad\text{or}\quad
4\in\{1+3,\ 2+2,\ 1+1+2,\ 1+1+1+1\},
$$
and
$$
6=6\quad\text{or}\quad
6\in\{1+5,\ 2+4,\ 3+3,\ 1+1+4,\ldots\}.
$$
The single-part case is simple. Every multi-part case represents an isogeny decomposition into simple factors. Each simple factor is classified by its endomorphism algebra, Rosati involution, Albert type, and Hodge group; one then checks whether relations between factors create additional mixed Hodge classes.

For dimension four, these decompositions and the relevant simple cases have already been analyzed. Dimension six follows the same structure but has more partitions and more admissible Albert subtypes. Known lower-dimensional results may settle individual factors, but the full sixfold still requires the product-group calculation above. The final target is a case table recording, for every subtype,
$$
\operatorname{Hg}(A),\qquad
\dim\mathcal B^p(A),\qquad
\dim\mathcal D^p(A),\qquad
\dim\mathcal E^p(A).
$$

For project management, the simple partition $6$ must be refined by its numerical Albert type. Write $e=[F:\mathbb Q]$ in Types I--III, and let $e_0$ be the degree of the maximal totally real subfield of the CM center in Type IV. $$
N_{\mathrm{work\ packages}}
=N_{\mathrm{simple}}+N_{\mathrm{non\text{-}simple}}

$$
